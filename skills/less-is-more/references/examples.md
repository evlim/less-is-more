# Decisions as features grow

These are illustrative patterns, not layouts to copy or measured outcomes.

## Destination landing page

**Task:** Choose where to travel, optionally refining data needs and trip duration.

Keep destination selection and search prominent. Direct country and region links are useful alternative entry points to the same outcome; they are not automatically competing CTAs. Optional data/days inputs belong with that task. Calendar selection and data-estimation help can open beside their inputs. A multi-destination feature can be a mode within search rather than a second hero.

Move unrelated education or promotional content out of the first decision area when it distracts. Do not hide the direct country links or force optional filters to become required steps. Preserve accessible labels when removing redundant headings.

The main path changes after navigation: results help users compare, and a specific plan action helps them continue to the provider. Carry the hierarchy through the journey rather than repeating the same CTA on every screen.

## Results with promotions and details

**Task:** Compare suitable choices and open one.

Keep distinguishing price, allowance, duration, and material limitations easy to scan. Use a consistent explicit action on each result. Several repeated actions are legitimate because each belongs to a different choice; do not arbitrarily highlight the first item as the preferred one.

A coupon-copy control can be quieter than the plan action while remaining legible and easy to use. Put supplementary detail behind a labeled disclosure. Keep a limitation that could change the decision visible in the comparison. Avoid a clickable whole card when it contains copy, selection, and detail controls with different behaviors.

## Analytics dashboard gains export and alerts

**Task:** Understand the selected report; sometimes investigate an exception.

Place export near the report with a restrained treatment. Put alert configuration in a predictable panel. Show an active critical alert clearly in context, even if it temporarily becomes the highest-priority item. Do not give every new tool a saturated toolbar button, or hide urgent status with optional configuration.

An expert dashboard can remain information-dense. Group related controls and remove duplicated emphasis rather than replacing useful content with empty space.

## Editor gains AI assistance

**Task:** Continue editing selected content.

Offer assistance near the selection or in an established tools panel. Preserve undo, save state, and existing shortcuts. Avoid a persistent animated promotion that competes with the document. Keep high-frequency commands directly accessible; an overflow menu is not automatically an improvement.

## Subscription screen is made simpler

**Task:** Understand and choose an account change.

Keep the total price, recurring term, consequences, and available cancel/refuse paths clear. A cleaner layout must not obscure alternatives or preselect an upgrade. Use spacing and grouping to explain choices; preserve equal prominence where required. If a change is destructive, make the consequence and recovery limits explicit before commitment.

## A small record for the next feature

Use the project's existing design notes. A compact entry might say:

> Destination landing: the primary outcome is choosing a destination. Search and direct country/region links are equivalent entry paths. Data and days are optional refinements. Assistance opens beside the relevant input. New trip features should integrate here before adding another prominent entry point. Revisit this when research changes the primary task.

Adapt this to the product. Record durable reasons, not a brittle count of buttons or colors.
